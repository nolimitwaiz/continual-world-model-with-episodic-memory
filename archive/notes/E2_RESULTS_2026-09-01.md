# CMWM-Synth-E2 Development Results

## Evidence boundary

These are complete five-condition, five-seed development results. They use the sealed validation
endpoint and do not access a confirmatory test split. They are suitable for deciding the next
experiment and for transparent research outreach, but not for a final confirmatory claim.

## Integrity audit

- 25/25 runs completed at 1,000 updates.
- Seeds: 11, 23, 47, 89, and 131.
- Every final artifact contains the same 512 unique queries in the same order.
- Every artifact has 64 examples in each of four tasks by two lags (64 and 128).
- All source, dataset, G0, endpoint-manifest, endpoint-seal, and cohort identities match.
- All recomputed prediction digests and final accuracies match their result artifacts.
- Test episodes and test access remain zero.

The preregistered oracle-advantage gate is not met. Oracle events do not provide a reliable
macro-LDMS advantage over fixed chunks or random-matched chunks on E2. C5 learned-boundary work
therefore remains closed under the current gate.

## What the result does establish

Bounded chunk memory provides a large, reproducible improvement over a 32-step context and a
64-record frame FIFO. Fixed chunks improve over context by 0.251563 LDMS, with a 95% paired-seed
interval of [0.240936, 0.262189], positive in all five seeds, and 71.8% relative error reduction.

This improvement is primarily causal-source retrieval. Context and frame FIFO score zero on both
causal-predecessor cells, while fixed and oracle chunks score 1.0. It is therefore evidence that
compressed long-term records preserve retrievable causal sources under the E2 delay/capacity
regime. It is not yet evidence for broad human-like episodic memory.

## Why the event-boundary claim did not clear

Four of eight cells are saturated at 1.0 for fixed, random-matched, and oracle conditions:
delayed-state at both lags and occlusion-continuity at both lags. These queries permit current-state
or persistent-state shortcuts and do not discriminate segmentation policies.

Oracle events show small secondary improvements over random-matched chunks in causal predecessor:
+0.040625 at lag 64 and +0.021875 at lag 128. Their unadjusted paired-seed intervals are positive,
but the eight-cell macro effect remains uncertain and these cellwise analyses are not corrected for
multiplicity. Object-location effects are inconsistent across seeds.

Mean validation LDMS plateaus by approximately steps 700--900 for every condition, so the null
macro result is not explained by prematurely stopped training.

## Efficiency

Mean elapsed time was 3,036 seconds for oracle events, 3,030 for random-matched chunks, and 3,775
for fixed chunks. Oracle events matched fixed-chunk quality while using about 19.6% less wall time
in this A100 development matrix. This is a useful secondary efficiency observation, not a hardware-
independent claim.

## Required next experiment

CMWM-Synth-E3 should preserve the successful sealed, paired protocol while making segmentation
necessary rather than merely available:

1. Change the queried state after the reference event so the current state cannot answer delayed
   recall or occlusion questions.
2. Include repeated, confusable event types and entity-role swaps within an episode.
3. Match storage bytes, record counts, source availability, and retrieval across fixed, random, and
   oracle conditions while varying only whether records respect event boundaries.
4. Add event-binding and temporal-order questions whose answers are destroyed by cross-event mixing.
5. Audit current-state, recency, entity, template, and action-source shortcuts before training.
6. Freeze a new balanced endpoint and rerun C0--C4 before opening learned boundaries.
