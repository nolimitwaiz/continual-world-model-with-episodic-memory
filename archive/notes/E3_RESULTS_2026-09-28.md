## What improves, and what does not

The largest descriptive improvements occur in delayed-state recall and occlusion continuity.
For example, occlusion at lag 128 averages 0.775 for events versus 0.321875 random and 0.328125
fixed. Location-history performance is similar to fixed chunks. Causal-source retrieval at lag
128 is worse for events (0.8875) than fixed chunks (0.95625). Thus this is a task-dependent
memory benefit, not uniform superiority. Cellwise intervals are descriptive and unadjusted.

The mean event curve rises from 0.604297 at step 700 to 0.735547 at step 1000; it is not demonstrably
converged. Fixed/random means are flatter. Any longer-training investigation should be separately
declared and matched across comparison conditions, with the original 1,000-update endpoint preserved.

## Scientific boundary and next decision

E3 was constructed after the E2 null result and known shortcut diagnosis. Report that chronology,
the E2 result and the E3 positive result together. Five-seed intervals describe seed variability,
not uncertainty over new worlds or people. The same validation questions are reused across seeds.
The experiment supplies correct event boundaries; it does not validate learned online boundaries.
The causal endpoint selects a source-containing record, not an exact action. Short-context/frame
controls can lack source records by design, so the meaningful segmentation contrast is C4 versus
C2/C3, not just C4 versus C0/C1.

This result supports independent, frozen held-out confirmation and mechanism controls next.
Learned segmentation still requires the existing G1/protocol gate; it is not automatically opened.
Diagnose seed variability and retention/segmentation confounds without tuning the sealed test.
No claim about all RAG systems, human-brain superiority, raw fMRI validation, true fast weights,
or a completed multimodal world model follows from these results. Chen behavioral analyses remain
a separate track. No new training was submitted during this status check.
